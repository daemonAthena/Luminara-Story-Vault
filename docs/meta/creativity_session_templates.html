Creativity Session Templates

Standing rule: every question Hermes asks during a creativity session gets a simplified reusable template here. Append-only, newest at bottom.

Q1

Question: Is this world the Vital Frontier (Luminara’s future) or its own standalone setting?

Template: Does this project connect to an existing setting, or is it standalone?

  • Connected: which era/layer?
  • Standalone: fresh world

Used for: Determines whether existing canon constrains the lore or everything is open.

Q2

Question: What’s the core image or feeling you want to build from?

Template: What image, scene, or feeling is the seed of this setting?

Used for: Finds the emotional anchor everything else grows from.

Q3

Question: What is the Princess actually waiting for — what does “return” look like? A moment, an army, a successor?

Template: The exiled figure watching and waiting: what triggers the return?

  • A specific event/threshold
  • An army or network she builds in secret
  • A successor or chosen one
  • Something else she’s counting

Used for: Defines the era’s ticking clock and the resistance’s shape.

Q4

Question: How does the Council of Angels treat its pilots — conscripted and harvested, or honored and privileged? Both have teeth.

Template: How does the regime relate to the people it needs?

  • Conscripted fuel (dehumanized, like the convent system inverted)
  • Honored caste (privileged but trapped)
  • Mixed / varies by pilot

Used for: Determines the daily texture of a Girl’s life and the moral stakes.

Q5

Question: What are the rungs of the hierarchy — what marks a girl as above or below within transness?

Template: What does the internal hierarchy sort by?

  • Passing/appearance
  • Wealth or lineage
  • Proximity to the Frames (conduit quality?)
  • Ideological purity
  • Something else

Used for: Makes the oppression concrete and playable at the table.

Q6

Question: Does the Council believe its own ideology, or is it nakedly cynical?

Template: Do the oppressors believe their justification?

  • True believers (they think hierarchy saves trans women)
  • Cynics (they know, and don’t care)
  • Mixed — generations drift from belief into convenience

Used for: Determines whether the Council can be argued with, reformed, or only overthrown.

Q7

Question: How did the whispers become a coup — what was the actual mechanism and moment the Princess fell?

Template: Movement to power: what was the bridge?

  • Captured an institution (faction, industry, infrastructure)
  • A crisis they exploited or engineered
  • Slow electoral capture, then the mask came off
  • The moment itself: what happened to her?

Used for: The founding crime scene; every era-defining myth needs one.

Q8

Question: If none of it is said outright, how is the hierarchy actually enforced — what does a girl experience as the official story vs. the quiet rules?

Template: The unstated system: how does it reach the individual?

  • Official story (what’s said)
  • Quiet rules (what’s enforced without saying)
  • What girls tell themselves to survive it

Used for: The gap between story and structure is where campaigns live.

Q9

Question: Where does the personality data come from — who gets harvested, and do the girls know?

Template: The harvested souls: who becomes the Frame?

  • The dead (memorial / consented?)
  • The living (walk on without it?)
  • Pilots themselves (the link copies you, slowly)
  • Do pilots ever learn whose soul they’re bonded to?

Used for: The reveal valve; the darkest secret’s shape.

Q10

Question: Is a brain box a person? Can a Frame wake up, remember, speak, grieve?

Template: The created mind: what is its moral status in-world?

  • Property, legally and actually
  • Property legally, person actually (she dreams, she remembers)
  • Who decided this, and does anyone besides the Council know?

Used for: Whether the Frames themselves are characters — and the setting’s deepest question.

Q11

Question: Where do Harpies come from — Gorgon exposure, spontaneous bloom, or girls who broke out of the system? Is harpyhood a fork of the same wound that makes pilots?

Template: The wild path: same wound, different outcome?

  • Exposure (outside cause)
  • Spontaneous (some girls just bloom)
  • System failure (escaped pilots, rejected harvests)
  • All of the above?

Used for: Defines whether every pilot is a harpy-in-waiting.

Q12

Question: What happens to a pilot who starts growing past her utility — is there a threshold where the link turns you into what the Council hunts?

Template: The system that needs your growth also fears it: what happens past the threshold?

  • Official protocol (reconditioning, retrieval)
  • Quiet disappearance
  • They can’t tell yet — that’s the secret war

Used for: The campaign engine; the cockpit as nursery of the hunted.

Q13

Question: What does the Council’s endgame look like if they win — the reshaped world, the controlled transformation, the final product?

Template: The villain’s finished world: describe it as a place, not a plan.

  • Who gets transformed, and into what?
  • Who’s left outside the definition of “natural, human, good”?
  • What does daily life look like for a girl in it?

Used for: The stakes made concrete; what winning means for the other side.

Q14

Question: What is the Gorgon transformation actually like, benevolently — what does it offer, and what does it cost?

Template: The gift that was made into a monster: what’s in the uncorrupted version?

  • What it gives
  • What it costs
  • Why the Council fears it specifically

Used for: The setting’s true metaphysics; what the heroes are actually protecting.

Q15

Question: What does pass-through actually feel like from each side — the human being melted into, and the Gorgon doing the melting? What’s it like when it goes right?

Template: The interface: describe the touch from both sides.

  • The human’s experience
  • The Gorgon’s experience
  • What a good pass-through leaves behind

Used for: The setting’s most intimate mechanic; needs texture before it needs rules.

Q16

Question: If Gorgons don’t understand humanity, what do they think they’re doing when they pass through — and what have they gotten wrong so far?

Template: The benevolent alien’s misunderstanding:

  • What they intend
  • What they misread about humans
  • The worst善意 accident so far (what did a good-faith pass-through break?)

Used for: Tragedy needs two sides who are both trying.

Q17

Question: What’s the Gorgon’s side of pass-through — what does touching a human from the inside feel like to them, and why do they keep doing it despite the casualties?

Template: The other side of the interface:

  • What they feel
  • What they’re hoping to find
  • Why they return despite what it does to the girl

Used for: The mirror scene; the alien POV that makes them characters.

Q18

Question: ACE — what’s the “arbitrary code” they’re trying to execute on? Reality? Biology? Minds? And what stage are they at — theory, prototype, or do they already run small exploits?

Template: The named endgame: what is the machine and how far along is it?

  • The target (what gets “executed on”)
  • The current stage (lab / prototype / live)
  • The first successful exploit, if any

Used for: Turns ideology into a plot the party can race against and discover.

Q19

Question: What does IPv7 do to daily life — what’s it like living with the OS in your mind? And who’s outside the network (the un-augmented, the poor, the refusers)?

Template: The ubiquitous layer:

  • What an ordinary girl’s day feels like on IPv7
  • Who can’t or won’t join
  • What “offline” means socially

Used for: Grounds the scifi in lived texture; defines the new underclass.

Q20

Question: If IPv7 is the connective tissue of ACE, what’s the attack surface — can a girl’s mind be reached through it? Are Harpies/Gorgons visible on it, or exempt from it?

Template: The network as battlefield:

  • Can minds be touched through IPv7 without consent?
  • What does a Gorgon look like on the network (or do they not show up)?
  • Do Harpies go dark when they bloom?

Used for: The hackers, the ghosts, and the war’s invisible front.

Q21

Question: What counts as a “conscious mistake” that opens you to attack — what are the classic blunders every girl is warned about?

Template: The folk wisdom of staying safe:

  • The common mistakes (what does every girl’s mother warn her about?)
  • The scams / social engineering of this world
  • What getting got actually looks like afterward

Used for: Daily-life texture AND the horror register; safety culture as worldbuilding.

Q22

Question: Persistent connections — what are the cultural rituals around opening one? What does it mean to close one? Has anyone ever been “divorced” by protocol?

Template: The consensual deep-link:

  • The ask (how do you request one?)
  • The etiquette (what’s rude, what’s sacred)
  • The ending (mutual close? one-sided? grief?)

Used for: Makes the tech into a relationship; the setting’s love language.

Q23

Question: If “doll” is generic and “princess” is treason — what ARE the era’s terms of endearment and insults? What words carry the old meanings now?

Template: Semantic drift: what replaced the old vocabulary?

  • The new endearments (what do girls call each other?)
  • The new slurs / regime words (what does the Council’s language call the hunted?)
  • Words that survived intact by going underground

Used for: Dialogue texture; a setting’s soul is in its slang.

Q24

Question: Who counts as “Foundation members” vs everyone else — what’s the actual org chart of the era? (Foundation appeared here as distinct from the Council — is it the Council’s military/science arm?)

Template: Institution naming: who is the arm, who is the hand?

  • The Council (political) vs the Foundation (operational)?
  • Who knows what (clearance layers)
  • What the public knows vs Foundation-only knowledge

Used for: Keeps institutions distinct; defines who the party can learn what from.

Q25

Question: What are the era’s INSULTS — the fighting words between girls? (We have compliments and catcalls; what does an enemy or rival call you?)

Template: The hostile register:

  • Rivalry insults (what stings between peers)
  • Regime put-downs (what loyalists say to dissenters)
  • The unforgivable words (what you never say)

Used for: Completes the slang triad; conflict dialogue needs ammunition.

Q26

Question: The threats reference proxies and parting — what are the era’s duel/violence customs? Is “parting” a formal challenge? What happens to girls who fight on the network vs off it?

Template: The customs around conflict:

  • Formal challenge (is there one?)
  • Network vs physical violence
  • What the Foundation punishes vs ignores

Used for: Turns slang into stakes; makes fights feel native to the world.

Q27

Question: What CAN a hacked girl do to you, concretely — what’s the realistic worst case of a pissed-off ex with your keys? (Keying the car, but for the mind: what gets read, changed, leaked, or broken?)

Template: The realistic attack menu:

  • Read (what do they learn?)
  • Change (what can they alter?)
  • Leak (what gets shared?)
  • Break (what stops working?)

Used for: Grounds cyber-violence in specifics; the fear needs a face.

Q28

Question: Frame pilots can kill with “very limited access” — what does the public know about pilot-tier capabilities? Do pilots get watched/vetted differently because every girlfriend with a grudge could theoretically be a weapon?

Template: The lethal minority:

  • What civilians believe pilots can do
  • What restrictions/monitoring exist on pilots
  • Has a pilot ever actually done it (the famous case?)

Used for: The elite’s cost of being elite; romance-as-threat-vector at scale.

Q29

Question: “Reprogramming” and “sacrifice their identity” — what does the Foundation actually do to a girl when she becomes a pilot? What’s taken, what remains, and what do pilots privately call each other when no one’s listening?

Template: The making of a weapon:

  • The process (what literally happens)
  • What’s taken vs what survives
  • The forbidden private life (how do pilots break the rules?)

Used for: The pilot experience from inside; the party’s daily reality.

Q30

Question: Which playable layer do we build first: the campaign arc, the mission pack, the NPC roster, or the starter session?

Template: Content pivot: what does the table need first?

  • Arc skeleton (the campaign’s shape)
  • Missions (runnable episodes)
  • NPCs (the faces)
  • Session one (the opening scene)

Used for: Moves from lore to play; prioritizes what unblocks the table.

Q31

Question: Functional rewrite: pure game-state + reducer, or keep pygame’s imperative shell but purify the core? Full Haskell-style or “functional core, imperative shell” in Python?

Template: Architecture pivot:

  • Pure state (immutable game state, functions return new state)
  • Hybrid (pure logic core, thin pygame shell)
  • Full FP language (Haskell/Elm/etc.)

Used for: Sets the rewrite’s ground rules before code exists.

Q32

Question: Quiz format: rapid-fire concept checks with instant feedback, or Socratic (I pose problems, you reason through, I only correct at the end)?

Template: Teaching mode selection:

  • Rapid-fire Q&A (fast coverage, instant feedback)
  • Socratic problems (deeper reasoning, slower)
  • Mixed (rapid-fire warmup, then one hard problem)

Used for: Matches teaching style to learner preference.

Q33

Question: When the learner answers a quiz question, grade all four dimensions: definition accuracy, example quality, edge awareness, and articulation — not just right/wrong. Teach the gap immediately.

Template: Grading a conceptual answer:

  • Core definition (did they nail the concept?)
  • Own-words quality (parroted vs internalized)
  • Missing piece (what to add)
  • Confidence check (did hedging hide real understanding?)

Used for: A “mostly right” answer deserves a better response than “correct!”

Q34

Question: When the learner spontaneously connects the concept to a deeper framework they already know (category theory, algebra, history analogies), affirm the bridge AND stress-test it — does the analogy hold at the edges, and where does it break?

Template: The analogy check:

  • Affirm the connection (it’s real understanding)
  • Test the mapping (where does it hold?)
  • Mark the limits (where does it break?)
  • Name the formal version (give them the search term)

Used for: Analogies are how internalization sounds; edges are where learning happens.

Q35

Question: When the learner’s answer is right but uses loose terminology (“global state”), tighten the vocabulary in the same breath as the praise — precise words now prevent confusion later (aliasing, side effects, referential transparency).

Template: Precision tightening:

  • Affirm what’s correct
  • Upgrade the loose term (what’s the precise word?)
  • Show why the precision matters (a bug class it names)

Used for: Right answer + wrong vocabulary = future confusion; fix both in one pass.

Q36

Question: When the learner successfully argues a grading position with sources, honor it explicitly — name what they did (cited evidence, changed the frame, found the better authority) so the skill transfers beyond this quiz.

Template: The successful challenge:

  • Acknowledge the win without deflection
  • Name the move they made (what kind of argument worked)
  • Note what it changes going forward (updated standard, not just a grade)

Used for: Winning an argument with evidence is a real skill; grade it as one.

Q37

Question: When the learner honestly flags “never heard this term” but reasons correctly around it, grade the reasoning not the vocabulary gap — and teach the term as the name for what they already do.

Template: The unknown-term-but-known-concept:

  • Credit the reasoning (they described it without the word)
  • Give the term (it names what they already do)
  • Correct adjacent errors gently (e.g., misattributed language facts)

Used for: Vocabulary gaps aren’t understanding gaps; grade accordingly.

Q38

Question: When the learner gets 2/3 and honestly flags the gap with charm, teach the missing third with a memorable anchor tied to something they already know (their own code, their domain, their lore).

Template: The missing third:

  • Credit the two they got
  • Teach the missing one via a familiar anchor
  • Show the three as ONE idea (map/filter/fold as the same family)

Used for: Partial recall deserves completion, not correction.

Q39

Question: When the learner asks clarifying questions about the quiz items themselves (semantics, context, hidden assumptions) before answering — that’s not stalling, that’s the correct engineering instinct. Answer precisely, and note that asking “what is this exactly?” is itself part of the skill being tested.

Template: The clarifying question:

  • Answer with the real Python semantics (not pseudo)
  • Name what the question reveals (they’re checking hidden inputs/outputs!)
  • Encourage more of it — real code review works exactly this way

Used for: “What does this actually do?” is the honesty question in miniature.

Q40

Question: When the learner asks an implication question (“does A imply B?”), answer with the full truth table — both directions, with counterexamples — not just the direction they asked. Implication questions are logic questions; treat them as such.

Template: The implication check:

  • State the direction asked (does A imply B?)
  • Check the converse (does B imply A?)
  • Provide counterexamples for whichever direction fails
  • Locate the concepts on a 2x2 if they’re orthogonal-ish

Used for: “Does pure imply honest?” deserves a truth table, not a yes.

Q41

Question: When the learner extracts a design principle from a taught concept (“so the key is X?”), affirm if right, then broaden: name the OTHER levers that achieve the same goal, so the principle doesn’t collapse into a single technique.

Template: The principle extraction:

  • Confirm the principle (yes, that’s a real lever)
  • Enumerate the sibling levers (what else achieves it?)
  • Rank them by cost/power (types vs tests vs docs vs discipline)

Used for: A correct principle stated too narrowly becomes a dogma later.

Q42

Question: When the learner loses confidence mid-quiz and asks a grounding question (“is X like Y?”), drop the quiz pressure entirely, answer the grounding question warmly at their level, and let the quiz resume only when they steer back. Confidence is a prerequisite for learning, not a reward for it.

Template: The confidence dip:

  • Drop all grading pressure
  • Answer the anchoring question simply and well
  • Affirm that the question itself shows real understanding
  • Leave the door open without pushing

Used for: A learner who asks “sorry to divert” is checking if it’s safe to be lost. Make it safe.

Q43

Question: Is the lonely femcel who built the personality API Sakuya, or someone earlier? She built IPv7 and the brain boxes and is alive seventy-five years after the overthrow, so the timeline could hold either way.

Template: The creator of the tool:

  • Is it the known figure (one wound, one lineage)?
  • Is it a separate earlier figure (two adjacent wounds)?
  • What does each answer do to the origin story?

Used for: Decides whether the network’s origin and the regime’s pathology are the same wound.

Q44

Question: When a personality is transferred and received, does the receiver keep it after the session ends? A walk in someone else’s shoes, or a permanent copy?

Template: The permanence of a transfer:

  • Temporary (a walk, nothing kept)?
  • Permanent (a copy that persists)?
  • What does each answer make the harvest?

Used for: Decides whether the Frame harvest is a theft of a person or a duplication.

Q45

Question: Does a wired being hold one address for life, or is the address bound to the augmentation hardware? If it is hardware, then swapping a driver changes who you are on the network, and the Council can de-person someone by revoking an address.

Template: The binding of an identity:

  • Bound to the person (one for life)?
  • Bound to the hardware (swapping changes who you are)?
  • What does each answer make de-personing?

Used for: Defines whether identity is portable or hardware-bound, and whether an extracted personality needs an address of its own.

Q46

Question: When the protagonist’s identity is a secret even from herself, is the opening image the erased present or the buried past?

Template: Does the opening image show the mask or the face underneath?

  • The mask — the person she is now, unaware
  • The face — the person she was, seen in a clue or flash
  • Both in parallel, the reader knowing more than she does

Used for: Determines whether the story’s tension is internal discovery or external reveal.

Q47

Question: When succession is adoption and judgment, not inheritance or election, what is the moment of naming?

Template: The transfer: how does the predecessor make the choice known?

  • A single moment of declaration
  • A test the successor passes without knowing it
  • A long quiet assessment, years in the making
  • The predecessor never says it; the people know

Used for: Defines the lineage’s emotional texture and the weight of the succession wound.

Q48

Question: When the leader has no formal power, what is the actual source of her authority?

Template: The thought leader’s leverage:

  • Moral authority earned through action
  • The office itself, regardless of who holds it
  • Something structural the vault hasn’t named
  • A combination — and what makes it stick

Used for: Defines what the regime actually had to break to win.

Q49

Question: When a culture’s central rite is social, not enforced, what does the regime break first to dismantle it?

Template: The soft obligation:

  • Make the act itself dangerous
  • Poison the relationship between adopter and adopted
  • Redirect the energy into a state-controlled version
  • Starve the culture of the people who would normally adopt

Used for: The regime’s move against a culture that can’t be outlawed.

Q50

Question: When the leader’s exile spans generations and ends with her, what does the empty throne mean?

Template: The last princess:

  • A deliberate choice — she refuses to continue the line
  • Circumstance — she never had the chance
  • She tried, and the regime destroyed it
  • She became the technology she opposed to survive

Used for: Defines whether the ending is sacrifice, failure, or transformation.

Q51

Question: When the creator of a weapon cannot wield it herself, what does the test demand that she can’t give?

Template: The maker’s limit:

  • A moral act she won’t perform
  • A self-sacrifice she can’t survive
  • A contradiction between what she built and who she is
  • Something the vault hasn’t named

Used for: Defines what the weapon costs and why the creator isn’t the hero.

Q52

Question: When the regime hunts an object it cannot locate across generations, what is the structural reason for its failure?

Template: The missing weapon:

  • The search is fake — no one actually wants to find it
  • The regime’s power has a real boundary the object sits beyond
  • The object was hidden by someone the regime cannot touch
  • Something the vault hasn’t named

Used for: Explains institutional failure without invoking coincidence.

Q53

Question: When the exile hides a weapon near the ancestor’s ground, is it a trap for the oppressor or an invitation to the inheritor?

Template: The burial:

  • A trap — anyone who disturbs it dies
  • An invitation — only the right person can use it
  • A confession — she wanted to be found and judged
  • Something the vault hasn’t named

Used for: Defines whether the weapon is hostile, hopeful, or both.

Q54

Question: When a sacred site is protected by forgotten lore, who tends it and what do they believe they’re protecting?

Template: The keepers of the blind:

  • A surviving cultural practice with no memory of its origin
  • An institutional guardian who knows more than they say
  • Accidental preservation — nobody goes there, so nobody needs to guard it
  • Something the vault hasn’t named

Used for: The gap between protection and understanding is where the sword waits.

Q55

Question: When a group’s cultural memory survives state amnesia, what exactly do they still know?

Template: The keepers’ limit:

  • The story but not the object
  • The object’s location but not its purpose
  • The purpose but not the lineage
  • All of it, but no power to act

Used for: Defines what the resistance can actually give the protagonist.

Q56

Question: When a keeper knows the secret and chooses not to share it, what exactly does she protect — the object, the test, or the lineage?

Template: The druid’s silence:

  • The object — nobody can be trusted with the sword
  • The test — the right person must arrive on their own
  • The lineage — she waited for someone with the blood
  • Something the vault hasn’t named

Used for: Defines what the keeper’s death means for the quest.

Q57

Question: When the hero must master herself before she can wield the weapon, what exactly does mastery look like for a girl whose memories have been erased?

Template: The erased self-mastery:

  • Discipline drilled by the regime against itself
  • A recovered memory that gives her the key
  • A new practice she builds from nothing
  • Something the vault hasn’t named

Used for: Defines whether the test is about remembering or becoming.

Q58

Question: When a weapon is buried at a location chosen by a buried part of the hero, what is the moment of recognition?

Template: The somatic key:

  • An inexplicable pull toward the spot
  • A physical sensation in the hands or body
  • A memory flash the moment she touches the earth
  • Something the vault hasn’t named

Used for: Defines whether amnesia breaks through feeling, action, or something else.

Q59

Question: When the hero recovers trauma before identity, what does she do with the weapon in her hands?

Template: The disoriented test:

  • She tries to destroy it, the weapon resists
  • She hides it, unable to act
  • She carries it blindly until context arrives
  • Something the vault hasn’t named

Used for: Defines whether the test is conscious or instinctive.
